## How to use this bank

- Begin a new full mock with a brief simulated interviewer introduction, then “Tell me about yourself.” Wait, coach, and invite a retry. This standard workshop opening is separate from the source bank. Select relevant bank examples alongside role-customized seeds, then generate new questions and follow-ups as practice continues. Seeds are not a fixed script or question cap; there is no mandatory bank/custom ratio. The short in-class exercise practices the opening and a retry.
- Preserve the original question ID and wording in the brief. Label a changed question "Adapted from IBM-XX"; do not label it verbatim.
- Questions IBM-02 and IBM-03 mention IBM/CDO. Adapt company references for a different employer and use only verified employer context. Do not imply these are that employer's actual interview questions.
- Questions IBM-23 and IBM-24 require looking at the portfolio. In voice-only practice, skip them or clearly adapt them to a spoken walkthrough. Do not claim the coach saw a page or screen it was not given.
- These questions are practice inputs, not a hiring rubric. IBM's examples do not become the target employer's requirements.
- Match the question to a competency, but let the student choose their story. Ask for missing facts rather than inventing an experience, conflict, result, or metric. A suggested answer may use confirmed facts only.
- Candidate-supplied answers are new self-reported evidence: ask for clarification when they conflict with existing sources. Do not assume every missing story is a weakness.

## Questions (source wording retained)


### IBM-01 | Motivation and team fit | PDF page 1

Why are you looking to make a job change?


### IBM-02 | Motivation and team fit | PDF page 1

Before this interview, what did you learn about our CDO organization that surprised or intrigued you? What questions did that raise for you?


### IBM-03 | Motivation and team fit | PDF page 1

What drew you to IBM in the first place and then to this specific role on our team? What feels exciting or meaningful about the work we do?


### IBM-04 | Motivation and team fit | PDF page 1

What kind of team culture brings out your best work? How do you contribute to building that kind of environment?


### IBM-05 | Collaboration and communication | PDF page 1

What tools or habits do you rely on to stay aligned with global teams when real-time collaboration isn’t possible? Can you share a specific example?


### IBM-06 | Collaboration and communication | PDF page 1

How do you make it easy for others—especially remote teammates—to give you what you need to move forward in your work? Can you share a technique or example?


### IBM-07 | Collaboration and communication | PDF page 1

Tell me about a time where you had to lead without formal authority. How did you get others to follow your direction or support your ideas?


### IBM-08 | Collaboration and communication | PDF page 1

Tell me about a time when a someone you needed to collaborate with was disengaged or unresponsive. How did you get them to participate?


### IBM-09 | Growth and learning | PDF page 1

Describe a situation where you had to learn something completely new in a short amount of time. How did you approach the learning curve?


### IBM-10 | Growth and learning | PDF page 1

Can you share an example of when you realized your current skill set wasn’t enough for a challenge you faced? What did you do to close the gap?


### IBM-11 | Growth and learning | PDF page 1

Tell me about a time when feedback made you uncomfortable or defensive. How did you move past that reaction and turn it into something constructive?


### IBM-12 | Growth and learning | PDF page 1

What’s something about our team or this role that feels like a stretch for you? What kind of support would help you succeed in that area?


### IBM-13 | Problem solving and decision making | PDF page 2

Describe a time you had to deliver something with really tight deadlines. How did determine where to start? How did you decide what was “good enough?”


### IBM-14 | Problem solving and decision making | PDF page 2

Tell me about a time when the problem you ultimately addressed turned out to be different than what was originally framed. How did you uncover the real issue?


### IBM-15 | Problem solving and decision making | PDF page 2

Describe a situation where you had to make a decision or take action without clear guidance. How did you navigate the ambiguity, and what did you learn?


### IBM-16 | Problem solving and decision making | PDF page 2

Have you ever worked on a project where the constraints felt unreasonable or constantly shifting? How did you stay focused and productive?


### IBM-17 | Impact and influence | PDF page 2

Tell me about a time when your design work directly contributed to a measurable business result. What was the need, and how did your work make a difference?


### IBM-18 | Impact and influence | PDF page 2

Can you share an example of a project where you connected user needs to business goals—and saw tangible results? What did you learn from that experience?


### IBM-19 | Impact and influence | PDF page 2

Describe a situation when you had to advocate for a design or strategy that others didn’t initially support or understand. How did you win them over?


### IBM-20 | Impact and influence | PDF page 2

Have you ever had to explain the business impact of your work to a non-designer? What did you say, and how did you make it resonate?


### IBM-21 | Creativity and process | PDF page 2

What’s the most unconventional solution you’ve ever proposed or implemented? How did you convince others to try it, and what was the result?


### IBM-22 | Creativity and process | PDF page 2

Think of a project where you had to make trade-offs between your ideal design and real-world constraints. How did you decide what was worth compromising? Is there anything you’ll never compromise on?


### IBM-23 | Creativity and process | PDF page 2

Please share your screen and load your portfolio website. How did you get feedback on your site and incorporate that into what I’m seeing now?


### IBM-24 | Creativity and process | PDF page 2

Please share your screen, load your portfolio, and let’s take a look at your __________ project together. [Ask about a specific project’s intention, process, deliverables, challenges, or outcomes.]

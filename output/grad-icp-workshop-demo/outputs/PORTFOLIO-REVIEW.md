**Scope:** This review covers copy only, not visual design, layout, or interaction.
**Target role:** Decagon Brand Design Lead (see `sources/decagon-brand-design-lead.md`)
**Reviewed text:** Opening paragraph and "A Shared Motion System" section (see `sources/optro-evidence.md`)
**Date:** 2026-10-26

---

## What these paragraphs are trying to do

The **Opening** establishes attribution (sole motion designer), scope (full
program from handoff to live execution), and collaboration (design team,
executives, stage production). The **Shared Motion System** paragraph is
the strongest systems-thinking proof in this case study: it shows the
candidate building reusable rules, not just executing one-off deliverables.
Together they serve the Decagon lens on "evolve a coherent brand system"
and "fast iteration across workstreams with craft."

## ICP check

### Craft bar

The opening credibly scopes the work — translating a visual identity into
motion across an entire event program. The systems paragraph strengthens
this by showing process, not just output. Neither paragraph can demonstrate
visual craft (copy-only scope), but both signal a designer who thinks in
infrastructure, not just deliverables.

### Attribution

Strong. "I was the only motion designer on the project" is clear and
verifiable against the credits line (`optro-evidence.md`, line 10). The
opening names the team without overclaiming. This directly addresses the
ICP's #1 pass-reason: "I can't tell what they specifically did."

### "Would I want them in the room?"

The opening signals someone who works across stakeholders and production.
The systems paragraph adds honest constraint-reasoning ("One person cannot
hand-animate a program this size in a month"). This reads as judgment, not
performance.

The Decagon posting asks for someone who can "evolve a coherent brand

## Three Prioritized Edits

### Edit 1 (High impact): Surface the systems claim earlier in the opening

**Current text:** "I was the only motion designer on the project. I
translated the visual identity into keynote sequences and speaker
introductions, then built a shared set of motion rules so every sequence in
the program moved the same way."

**Problem:** The strongest Decagon signal — "built a shared set of motion
rules" — is buried in the second sentence, after role and timeline context.
A Decagon reader scanning for systems evidence in 10 seconds may not reach
it.

**Suggested revision:** Move "built a shared set of motion rules so every
sequence in the program moved the same way" to the front of the second
sentence, before "I translated the visual identity into keynote sequences."

**Risk:** Slightly less chronological. Acceptable tradeoff for this reader.

### Edit 2 (Medium impact): Qualify "agent-assisted prototypes" with specificity

**Current text:** "then used agent-assisted prototypes to apply them across
the program"

**Problem:** The evidence boundary (`optro-evidence.md`, line 44) permits
"agent-assisted prototypes" but not "AI-generated motion." The current
phrasing is honest but vague — a Decagon reader who values AI curiosity (a
noted strength per `ICP.md`, line 111) gets no signal of what the tools
actually did.

**Open question for Micah:** What did the agent-assisted prototypes
actually do in this workflow? The source establishes that prototypes were
agent-assisted and that final motion was refined by hand, but it does not
describe the agent's specific role (e.g., generating initial keyframes,
producing timing variations, laying out sequences from the guidelines).
A one-clause qualifier grounded in what actually happened would strengthen
the AI-curiosity signal without overstepping.

**Risk:** Any qualifier must stay within what actually occurred. Do not
imply the final output was automated.

### Edit 3 (Lower impact): Name what "motion rules" means concretely

**Current text:** "shared guidelines for timing, easing, and sequencing"

**Problem:** "Timing, easing, and sequencing" is accurate but generic — it
could describe any motion-design brief. One concrete example would make the
systems claim feel testable rather than abstract, strengthening the Decagon
"evolve a coherent brand system" signal.

**Open question for Micah:** Can you confirm a specific motion convention
(e.g., a particular easing curve, a transition pattern, a timing rule) that
unified the program? The current source does not name one. Any example
added here would be new copy that needs your verification before use.

**Risk:** Adding specifics the source doesn't currently support. Any
addition must be confirmed and then recorded in `optro-evidence.md`.
